Tracing ethical risk from mine to market

Gold is small, valuable, and easy to melt and recast. Once refined and mixed, its origin can become exceptionally difficult to establish—and may be effectively erased.

02 · Where harm can enter

Five risks to keep in view

These risks are not present in every operation or shipment. The point of due diligence is to identify and respond to risk, not to assume that every gold source is harmful.

Child and forced labor

Labor exploitation can occur in high-risk mining and related work, particularly where work is informal, hazardous, or weakly monitored. Gold may then pass through intermediaries before buyers can see who mined it.

MineTraders & Exporters

Mercury pollution and health harm

Some ASGM processes use mercury to recover gold, creating potential exposure and environmental contamination. Impacts depend on the methods used and how mercury and waste are handled.

MineTraders & Exporters

Conflict financing by armed groups

In some conflict-affected settings, armed groups may tax, control, or profit from mineral production and trade. A shipment that crosses borders or changes hands can make those links harder for downstream buyers to detect.

MineTraders & ExportersRefinery

Money laundering and sanctions evasion

Gold's value density, portability, and tradability can make it attractive for moving or disguising value. Complex counterparties, false paperwork, or opaque ownership can make illicit finance and sanctions risks harder to screen.

Traders & ExportersVaults & Markets

The “recycled gold” loophole

Recycled material can be a legitimate source, but the label alone does not establish a clean history. If an upstream source is falsely presented as scrap or recycled gold, a buyer may overlook the original mine-related risks.

03 · Rules and their reach

A patchwork of expectations

These frameworks differ in legal force, covered actors, and scope. The summaries below are intentionally high-level; consult the primary texts for compliance decisions.

OECD Due Diligence Guidance for Responsible Supply Chains of Minerals from Conflict-Affected and High-Risk Areas Companies in mineral supply chains sourcing from conflict-affected or high-risk areas; it is an international due-diligence framework. A risk-based process to identify, prevent, mitigate, and account for adverse impacts in mineral supply chains. Across the chain, from upstream sourcing through downstream buyers. Guidance itself is not a universal, directly enforceable law; implementation and assurance vary.
LBMA Responsible Gold Guidance Gold refiners in the LBMA Good Delivery system and participating market actors within its programme. Responsible-sourcing due diligence and independent assurance under the LBMA programme. Especially refiners, with expectations reaching their supply chains. It is a market programme, not a law covering every gold producer, refiner, or buyer.
US Dodd-Frank Act Section 1502 Certain SEC-reporting issuers whose products use conflict minerals necessary to functionality or production. Disclosure and supply-chain due diligence concerning whether covered minerals may originate in the Democratic Republic of the Congo or adjoining countries and may benefit armed groups. Company sourcing and disclosure, including relevant gold supply chains. It is a disclosure regime with defined issuer, mineral, product, and geographic scope—not a general ban on conflict minerals.
EU Conflict Minerals Regulation EU importers of tin, tantalum, tungsten, and gold, within the regulation's scope and thresholds. Supply-chain due diligence for covered mineral imports from conflict-affected and high-risk areas. Importers and upstream sourcing, including gold refiners and smelters in the covered supply chain. It covers specified minerals and importers; it does not cover every gold transaction or downstream product.

04 · Tools, not truth machines

What technology can—and cannot—show

Each method answers a different question. None independently guarantees that a gold bar is ethical or that its recorded origin is true.

Supply chain mapping

Connects suppliers, facilities, routes, and stated custody records.

Can help prove

  • Which parties and handoffs a company has identified.
  • Where records or supplier disclosures are missing.

Cannot prove

  • That a disclosed supplier or document is truthful without corroboration.
  • That an unrecorded actor never handled the material.

Chemical / isotopic fingerprinting

Compares a sample's measured characteristics with reference material or known patterns.

Can help prove

  • Whether a sample is consistent with some reference signatures.
  • Whether samples may differ or share measurable characteristics.

Cannot prove

  • A unique mine of origin in every case; signatures can overlap or be altered by processing.
  • Ethical labor conditions or an unbroken custody record.

Blockchain traceability

Can create a shared, tamper-evident record of submitted events and attestations.

Can help prove

  • That recorded entries were not subsequently changed within a system.
  • Which participants entered or endorsed recorded events.

Cannot prove

  • That source data was true when entered or that every handoff was recorded.
  • Physical identity after gold is mixed or melted without separate verification.

Satellite monitoring

Uses imagery to observe changes in land cover and visible activity around mining areas.

Can help prove

  • That visible landscape changes or activity patterns occurred in a location.
  • Where further field checks may be useful.

Cannot prove

  • Who produced a specific consignment or what conditions workers faced.
  • That all mining activity is visible at the resolution and times observed.

AI risk screening

Can sort records and flag patterns for human review using selected data and criteria.

Can help prove

  • That particular records matched a screening rule or risk signal.
  • Where analysts may prioritize follow-up questions.

Cannot prove

  • That a flagged relationship is wrongdoing or that an unflagged source is safe.
  • That underlying data is complete, current, or unbiased.

The core limitation: data entered at the source can be false, and mixing or melting breaks physical traceability. Technology is strongest when records are independently checked and tied to accountable people and processes.

05 · Reflection

My takeaway

The refinery is a critical chokepoint, but the recycled-gold category can be an especially weak link when a label substitutes for evidence of origin. Technology can make gaps more visible; it is most useful when combined with independent audits, credible regulation, and people willing to act on what the evidence shows.
